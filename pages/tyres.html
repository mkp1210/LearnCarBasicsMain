Understanding Tyre Safety & Pressures

Your tyres are the only mechanical contact points between your automobile and the pavement. Operating with low air pressure patterns decreases your overall fuel tracking efficiency, ruins handling responsiveness, and increases catastrophic blowout failure risks under highway friction loads. Check your factory pressure ratings using the information card sticker embedded on your driver's side door jamb.

Step-by-Step: Checking Your Tyre Metrics

1. Ensure your tyres are "cold" (meaning the car has been parked for at least three hours or driven less than a mile).
2. Unscrew the plastic valve cap from the tyre stem.
3. Press your tyre pressure gauge firmly onto the valve stem until the escaping air hissing sound stops.
4. Read the measurement on the gauge screen or mechanical stick slider (measured in PSI or Bar).
5. Compare this number against your car's recommended PSI placard. If it matches, replace the cap; if it is low, add air at a local service station.

Difficulty Level: Intermediate

Emergency Roadside Tyre Swap Procedure

Getting a flat tyre on the road can be stressful, but knowing the precise mechanical steps to change it safely keeps you moving without waiting hours for roadside assistance. Always ensure you carry a functional jack, a lug wrench, and an inflated spare wheel in your vehicle trunk at all times.

Step-by-Step: Swapping a Blown Tyre

1. Ensure the car rests on flat ground away from highway lanes, switch on hazard indicators, and set the emergency brake system firmly.
2. Use the factory lug wrench to loosen the wheel bolts by a single turn counter-clockwise while the tyre is still on the asphalt.
3. Align the structural hydraulic or scissor jack under the designated frame weld lift notch point, and safely raise the car body frame.
4. Unbolt the lug nuts completely, slide the damaged flat tyre off the chassis hub assembly, and slide the replacement spare wheel on.
5. Tighten the lug nuts manually in a star sequence cross pattern, lower the jack safely, and torque the bolts down tightly with the wrench tool.
